## 3. Key facts both of you must know (likely professor questions)

**Origins and Republic**
- Founding date 753 BCE is traditional/legendary (Romulus and Remus). Archaeology shows settlement on the Palatine Hill in the 8th century BCE, but the date itself is a Roman tradition.
- Republic began 509 BCE (expulsion of the last king, Tarquin the Proud, by tradition).
- Government: two annually elected **consuls**; the **Senate** (about 300 members) advised; **assemblies** and **tribunes of the plebs** represented ordinary citizens. The **Twelve Tables** (c. 450 BCE) were the first written Roman law.
- **Punic Wars** (264-146 BCE) against Carthage; Rome destroyed Carthage in 146 BCE. Hannibal crossed the Alps in the Second Punic War.
- Julius Caesar crossed the Rubicon in 49 BCE and was assassinated on the Ides of March, 44 BCE.

**Empire**
- Octavian won the civil wars (Actium, 31 BCE), received the title **Augustus** in 27 BCE. He was **princeps**, not officially "king."
- **Pax Romana**: roughly 27 BCE to 180 CE. Greatest extent under **Trajan**, 117 CE.
- Division into East and West in 395 CE (after Theodosius I died). Diocletian earlier split administration (Tetrarchy, 284-305 CE).
- Be ready: *Republic vs. Empire?* Republic = shared, elected offices with checks; Empire = effectively one-person rule behind republican forms.

**Daily life**
- Classes: patricians, equestrians, plebeians, freedmen, enslaved people. Women could not vote or hold office but could own property and, in some cases, run businesses.
- **Paterfamilias** = male head of household with legal authority.
- Gladiators: mostly enslaved people, prisoners, or condemned criminals; some volunteers. "Bread and circuses" is from the poet **Juvenal**.

**Architecture and engineering**
- Colosseum (Flavian Amphitheater): begun under Vespasian, opened under Titus in 80 CE; about 50,000 spectators.
- Concrete (opus caementicium) used volcanic ash (pozzolana). **Pantheon dome** about 43 m diameter; built under Hadrian (c. 118-125 CE).
- Aqueducts relied on gravity; Pont du Gard is in southern France. Roads connected the empire (famous example: the Appian Way).

**Art and culture**
- Greek influence: Romans copied Greek sculpture (many Greek originals are known only through Roman copies).
- Vesuvius erupted in 79 CE and buried Pompeii and Herculaneum, preserving wall paintings and mosaics.
- Key writers: Virgil (*Aeneid*), Cicero, Livy, Ovid, Horace. Latin gave rise to the Romance languages.

**Religion**
- Capitoline Triad: Jupiter, Juno, Minerva. Greek equivalents: Zeus, Hera, Athena. Mars = Ares.
- Roman religion was ritual and state-centered. Emperor cult grew under Augustus and later emperors.
- **Edict of Milan 313 CE** (Constantine and Licinius) = toleration, *not* making Christianity the official religion. **Edict of Thessalonica 380 CE** (Theodosius I) = Nicene Christianity as state religion.

**Fall and legacy**
- Traditional end of the West: **476 CE**, Odoacer deposes Romulus Augustulus. Visigoths sacked Rome in 410 CE; Vandals in 455 CE.
- Causes are debated; avoid saying "one cause." The Eastern (Byzantine) Empire lasted until the fall of Constantinople in **1453**.
- Legacy: law, language, republican ideas, architecture, Christianity's institutional spread.

**Likely tough questions**
1. *Did Rome really "fall"?* The West's political structure ended; many institutions, language, and law continued; the East lasted another thousand years.
2. *Were Romans just copying the Greeks?* They borrowed heavily but adapted: concrete, arches, portraiture, and law are distinctly Roman.
3. *Is the Capitoline Wolf ancient?* The twins were added in the Renaissance; some scholars have argued the wolf itself may be medieval. Our slide says only that the twins are Renaissance additions.
